01 — The Settlement Control Room

Designing the infrastructure that gives Finance clarity, authority and confidence over money in transit.

01

Beyond the transaction

Most people think payments end when a transaction succeeds. For the Finance team, that’s where the work begins.

Every successful transaction creates a responsibility: making sure the right amount of money reaches the right destination at the right time. As Payaza grew, transaction volumes increased, settlement channels expanded, and operational complexity multiplied.

Some numbers for context

  1. 16+Currencies
  2. $30Bn+Total payment volume (TPV)
  3. 7+Products, services & offerings

02

Designing for operational clarity

A successful transaction is only the beginning. What happens after it needs the same level of clarity and control.

A successful transaction sets more in motion than a payment. It creates a series of settlement activities that Finance must monitor, reconcile, and ultimately bring to completion.

As Payaza’s payment operations grew, this became increasingly difficult to manage across fragmented sources and processes. The team needed a unified view that made the state of every settlement clear — and gave them the control to act when something needed attention.

How might we

…give Finance a clear view of every settlement, so they can understand what’s happening and confidently act when it matters?

03

Mapping the settlement engine

Systems first, interfaces later. That’s how you build a structure you can scale on.

Settlement sat at the intersection of Finance, Engineering and Product, with each team bringing a different perspective on how it should work. Finance understood the operational requirements. Engineering understood the technical constraints. The challenge was bringing both together into a settlement model everyone could work with.

“The best work happens when different perspectives become one clear direction.”

04

A shared language for money in transit

Before designing a single screen, we agreed on what a settlement is — and the states it moves through.

Collections are pooled into batches, and every batch moves through the same four states. Any state can branch into an exception, and every exception has an owner and a way back. That one model became the tabs and filters Finance works with, and the statuses Engineering builds against.

  1. 01

    Aggregating

    Collections land and are pooled into batches for each merchant.

  2. 02

    Awaiting

    The batch is closed and waiting for its settlement window.

  3. 03

    Ongoing

    Payout is initiated to the merchant’s bank and tracked until confirmed.

  4. 04

    Completed

    Funds are confirmed, posted and reconciled.

Exceptions

  • Paused
  • Flagged for fraud check
  • Payout failed

Can branch from any state — each one has an owner, an action and a way back into the flow.

05

Viewing the settlement lifecycle

A centralised workspace that gives the Finance team visibility and control over settlement activity. They can monitor settlement progress, manage collections, intervene when needed, and oversee the end-to-end settlement lifecycle — from aggregating to awaiting, ongoing and completed.

06

Settlement details & reconciliation

Finance teams can drill into individual settlements with a complete view of collections, payouts, fees, batches and settlement status. This gives greater visibility for tracking, reconciliation, and resolving settlement exceptions.

07

Designing for exceptions

The happy path runs itself. A control room earns its name when something goes wrong.

When a payout times out or a batch stalls, Finance needs three answers fast: what happened, what it affects, and what they’re allowed to do about it. The exception view puts all three on one screen — the cause in plain language, the batches involved, the actions available, and a full trail of who did what.

Key decisions

  1. One vocabulary for Finance and Engineering

    The four settlement states became the tabs, the filters and the system statuses. When Finance says “awaiting”, Engineering knows exactly what that means.

  2. Put actions where the problem is

    Clearing collections, pausing a settlement or running end of day happens from the row or the detail page — not from a separate admin tool Finance has to go looking for.

  3. Authority with accountability

    Anything that moves money goes through maker–checker, and every intervention lands in the activity trail. Finance gets the power to act, and the business gets a record of every action.

  4. Make the numbers add up on one screen

    Collections, fees, reserved amounts and payout sit side by side, so reconciling a settlement means reading it, not rebuilding it in a spreadsheet.

Impact · within 3 months of rollout

95%

faster settlement resolution. What used to take up to 2 working days to trace and confirm now takes less than an hour — Finance isn’t waiting on money in transit anymore.

55%More compliant

Non-compliant submissions fell sharply, reducing the need for manual audits.

90%Less time on task

Automating manual steps like getting access permissions and locating materials cut the time needed to complete a submission.

Reflection

The hardest design work happened before a single screen existed: getting Finance and Engineering to agree on what a settlement actually is. Once that model was shared, most interface decisions followed from it.

Next, I’d move the control room from reactive to proactive — alerting Finance when a settlement sits past its window instead of waiting for someone to notice, and producing reconciliation reports that are ready for month-end close.
